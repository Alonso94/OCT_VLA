# Object-centric conditioning for VLA policies: what 13 training runs measured

Status report, 2026-09-21. Covers the leftmost-target corpus and every cell run
against it: five action encodings, four backbones, three conditioning
mechanisms, and a compositional-generalisation sweep over object count.

---

## 1. Summary

The project asks whether conditioning a policy on object-centric scene state
helps over RGB alone. After 13 training runs and 25 closed-loop cells
(500 episodes), the position is:

**One configuration completes the task.** ACT — 80 M parameters, trained from
scratch — with **absolute end-effector actions**: 6/20 episodes succeed, mean
1.00 transfers. Every other cell in the project is at or near zero.

**Action encoding dominates backbone scale.** Holding everything else fixed,
changing the action space from absolute joint to absolute EE moves success from
0/20 to 6/20 (paired exact McNemar, *p* = 0.031). Changing the backbone from
80 M ACT to 3.16 B GR00T, at the same encoding, does not move it off zero.

**Object conditioning shows no measurable effect** on the one substrate where a
difference could appear, by any of the three mechanisms tried.

**Offline loss does not predict closed-loop success here.** Eight cases now,
including two cells within 10 % of each other offline that differ 6.7× online.
Validation loss should not be used to rank cells or select checkpoints in this
project without saying so explicitly.

A caveat that applies to every number below: **one training seed per cell,
20 evaluation seeds per profile.** Only two contrasts in the whole study reach
*p* < 0.05. The rest are reported as unresolved, not as null results.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Action encoding — ACT, RGB + proprioception

| encoding | offline L1 | success | ≥1 transfer | ≥1 lift | mean transfers | infeasible |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **absolute EE** | 0.0785 | **6/20** | **7/20** | **17/20** | **1.00** | 137 |
| absolute joint | 0.0773 | 0/20 | 3/20 | 15/20 | 0.15 | 0 |
| absolute joint, privileged | 0.1067 | 0/20 | 3/20 | 13/20 | 0.15 | 0 |
| joint delta | 0.1427 | 0/20 | 1/20 | 2/20 | 0.05 | 0 |
| EE delta | 0.1851 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0.00 | 67 |

Absolute EE against absolute joint: **success *p* = 0.031**, transfer
*p* = 0.344 (paired exact McNemar, same 20 seeds).

This overturns the project's prior standing convention, which held that
absolute joint targets were "the only encoding that has produced a non-zero
result." That was true on the pre-fix corpus and is no longer true.

The two arms are statistically indistinguishable offline (0.0785 vs 0.0773) and
differ 6.7× on mean transfers. Absolute EE pays 137 infeasible IK steps for it —
the intrinsic cost of acting in task space, recorded rather than hidden.

### 3.2 Backbone — absolute joint, RGB, identical corpus

| backbone | params | offline L1 (offsets 0–24) | success | ≥1 transfer | ≥1 lift |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ACT (from scratch) | 0.08 B | 0.0665 | 0/20 | **3/20** | **15/20** |
| SmolVLA | 0.45 B | 0.1713 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 5/20 |
| GR00T N1.7 | 3.16 B | **0.0578** | 0/20 | 1/20 | 12/20 |
| pi0.5 | 3.62 B | 0.1029 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 5/20 |

Offline normalised to offsets 0–24, since GR00T emits 25 rows against ACT's 50
and a shorter horizon is easier.

**No pretrained VLA beats an 80 M ACT trained from scratch.** GR00T has the best
offline action prediction in the project — 14.7× the constant predictor, 13 %
better than ACT — and converts it into one transfer in twenty episodes. All 60
VLA episodes end at the step limit: no crashes, no IK failures, the arms move
and never finish.

Against ACT's best cell (absolute EE), GR00T's best cell differs at **success
*p* = 0.031**, transfer *p* = 0.070.

The VLA cells ran 8 k steps against ACT's 40 k and are single-seed and
untuned. They are matched on data and encoding, not on compute. The fair
reading is *"these VLAs, LoRA-finetuned at this budget, do not beat ACT here"* —
not *"VLAs cannot do this task."*

### 3.3 Object conditioning — ACT, absolute EE

Same policy, same encoding, same corpus and seed; differing only in what is
observed.

| arm | observes | offline L1 | success | ≥1 transfer | mean transfers |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A5 | RGB + proprioception | 0.0785 | **6/20** | 7/20 | **1.00** |
| C2 | RGB + proprioception + objects | 0.0775 | 2/20 | **10/20** | 0.85 |
| C3 | proprioception + objects, no cameras | 0.1065 | 0/20 | 1/20 | 0.05 |

Object tokens move the two headline metrics in **opposite directions** and
neither difference survives testing: success *p* = 0.219, transfer *p* = 0.375.
At n = 20 this study cannot distinguish A5 from C2.

**C3 is the more decisive cell.** Object state *without* cameras collapses. In
joint space the equivalent arm matched its RGB counterpart exactly (3/20
transfers each), so privileged scene state alone was sufficient there and is
not sufficient here. Whatever the EE-space policy uses to succeed comes from
the images.

Note that 88 of the 120 conditioning dimensions have **zero variance** — only 3
of 8 token slots are occupied in a `three_object` scene, the rest zero-padded.
This is the first thing to check before concluding object state is
uninformative.

### 3.4 Compositional generalisation — 2 / 3 / 4 objects

Trained on `three_object` only; evaluated on all three profiles, 20 seeds each.

| arm | 2 objects | 3 objects (trained) | 4 objects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RGB | 0/20 succ, 0.10 tr | **6/20 succ, 1.00 tr** | 0/20 succ, 0.20 tr |
| RGB + objects | 1/20, 0.15 | 2/20, 0.85 | 0/20, **0.45** |
| objects only | 0/20, 0.00 | 0/20, 0.05 | 0/20, 0.00 |

**Neither arm transfers its competence off the trained object count.** Success
collapses to ~0 at both 2 and 4 objects.

One suggestive difference: at four objects the conditioned arm reaches ≥1
transfer in **9/20** episodes against RGB's 4/20, and mean transfers 0.45 vs
0.20 — it degrades more gracefully as the scene grows, despite being worse at
the trained count. This is the only place in the project where the object arm
has looked better than RGB on a partial-credit measure. It is single-seed and
n = 20; it points the right way for a compositionality claim without
establishing one.

Two objects being *harder* than three is counterintuitive and unexplained. Both
vision arms lift objects (0.55 / 0.50 mean) and then fail to place them. A
rollout video should precede any theory.

### 3.5 ControlVLA mechanism — GR00T, absolute EE

| cell | injection | offline L1 | success (2/3/4 obj) | ≥1 transfer | infeasible |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| F0 | none (RGB baseline) | **0.0706** | 0 / 0 / 0 | 5 / 2 / 0 | 23 / 22 / 21 |
| F1 | `controlvla` | 0.0955 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 5 / 2 / 13 |
| F2 | `pooled` | 0.0711 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 / 1 | 354 / 310 / 351 |

**Zero successes across all 180 episodes. This comparison measures nothing
about the mechanism**, because the baseline it sits on is itself at zero: you
cannot detect whether conditioning helps on top of a policy that never
succeeds. F0 failed the gate the experiment matrix specifies.

GR00T does not work on absolute EE either. The encoding that carries ACT to
6/20 gives GR00T 0/20.

The implementation is verified correct but untested for efficacy: both modes
pass a smoke test on the real 3.16 B model, the zero-init gradient check
confirms only V_z moves at step 0 (2/11 tensors, exactly as the mathematics
requires), and all 11 object tensors left zero during training. It works; it had
no substrate on which to help.

### 3.6 Offline loss versus closed-loop success

| pair | offline | closed-loop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 vs A5 | 0.0773 vs 0.0785 (1.6 % apart) | 0.15 vs 1.00 transfers (6.7×) |
| A1 vs B2 | 0.0665 vs 0.0578 (GR00T better) | 3/20 vs 1/20 transfers (ACT better) |
| F0 vs F1 | 0.0706 vs 0.0955 (F0 better) | both 0/20 |
| A5 vs C2 | 0.0785 vs 0.0775 (C2 better) | 6/20 vs 2/20 success (A5 better) |

**No cell in the project beats the persistence baseline** ("repeat the current
state") on offline L1 — including on the delta encodings, where persistence
means predicting the previous increment. Every cell beats the constant
predictor. Offline L1 is useful as a *floor* check and useless as a *ranking*.

## 4. Defects found, and what they invalidated

Six defects surfaced during this work. Four invalidated measurements that had
already been reported.

| defect | effect | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **uint8 images at evaluation** | Any policy declaring `VISUAL=IDENTITY` — pi0.5, SmolVLA, GR00T — was handed uint8 where training used float32/255. pi0.5 casts to float without dividing, then computes `img*2-1`, landing in **[-1, 509]** instead of [-1, 1]. Silent. **Every pi0.5 rollout before this ran on those inputs.** | fixed `41fc008`; pi0.5 re-evaluated and still scores zero, so the bug does not explain its failure |
| **hardcoded encodings at evaluation** | A dataset whose action column has no motor names — the 14-d EE-delta view — fell to a branch hardcoding `measured_aperture` against a `binary_command` dataset | fixed `41fc008` |
| **gripper validator ended episodes** | Under binary targets an L1 head overshoots [0,1] routinely; `ArmAction` rejected it and killed the episode. `Action.from_vector` now saturates, as hardware does | fixed `41fc008` |
| **SmolVLA conditioning dead at evaluation** | Plugins wrapped `forward` and `predict_action_chunk`; SmolVLA's `select_action` calls `_get_action_chunk` directly, so the object path was inert at rollout while training normally. Would have read as "conditioning did not help" | fixed `ec5885f`, wrapped at the mixin |
| **GR00T padded-action dilution** | GR00T pads actions to `max_action_dim=132` against our 16, so its *training loss* averages 116 columns of constant zero — roughly 8× dilution. Its reported loss is not comparable to any other backbone's | diagnostic fixed `41fc008`; the loss remains incomparable by construction |
| **`observation.object_tokens` typed as STATE** | LeRobot types any `observation.*` key as STATE, so every "RGB" cell lists the tokens in `input_features`. **Checked: not a leak** — `robot_state_feature` matches the exact name `observation.state` and ACT's forward touches only `OBS_STATE` and `OBS_ENV_STATE`. The baselines are genuine | verified, no change needed |

## 5. ControlVLA: what the paper specifies, and what we had

Re-checked against arXiv:2506.16211 on request. The implementation in this repo
was **not** the published method.

| | paper | our previous implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mechanism | `softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V + softmax(QK_zᵀ/√d)V_z` | `action_emb + injection(pool(Z))` |
| object tokens | kept as a **set**, used as keys/values | **pooled to one vector** |
| injection sites | every cross-attention block | one site |
| zero-init | the added **K_z, V_z** projections | the final `injection` linear |
| depends on the action token | yes | **no** — same bias added to every token |

The zero-init comparability guarantee was faithful. The function class was not.
The paper's added term is a content-dependent readout: action token *i* at chunk
step *t* computes its own weights over the objects. A broadcast bias cannot
attend to the grasp object and the place target at different points in one
50-step chunk, which is what a pick-and-place task needs.

This also explains the dead shuffled-token control. It is a no-op not merely
because the encoder is permutation-invariant, but because the set is destroyed
before it reaches the action stream — no per-object structure survives to be
scrambled.

**Now implemented** (`af9fc5f`): `object_injection_mode` selects `"controlvla"`
(published) or `"pooled"` (previous, retained so existing checkpoints load and
the two can be compared directly). Verified: zero-init identity, per-action-token
variation 2.36 where pooled is exactly 0, padding masked, empty scene finite.

**Still divergent:** the paper injects at every cross-attention block; we inject
at the single action-embedding seam every backbone shares. The paper also uses a
29 M DiT with ~5 M trained parameters and 10–25 demonstrations, against our
3 B-class backbones and 84 runs.

### Borrowed from Alfa-d

`object_entity_positional` adds a learned code across entity slots, off by
default. Alfa-d's entity encoders carry the same flag for the same purpose — an
order-sensitivity diagnostic, default-off to keep the encoder
permutation-invariant. **This gives the project a valid shuffled-token control
for the first time**; `validate_object_tokens` now warns when shuffle is set
without it. Alfa-d's own spec explicitly disclaims "compositional generalisation
from an entity encoder alone," which §3.4 is consistent with.

Alfa-d zero-pads to the max entity count and no encoder builds a key-padding
mask; ours masks explicitly, and an all-empty scene unmasks one zeroed slot so
softmax cannot return NaN.

## 6. Recommendations

Ordered by information per GPU-hour.

**1. Replicate A5 across seeds — ~5 GPU-h. Do this first.**
Every conclusion in this report rests on one training run scoring 6/20. Three
seeds would tell us whether the project's single working configuration is real
or a lucky draw. Nothing else is worth planning until this is known.

**2. Test ControlVLA on ACT, not on a VLA — ~4 GPU-h, needs a `control_act`
plugin.**
The mechanism comparison can only be run where a difference can appear, and
that is ACT. `modeling_act.py:497` builds `decoder_in` as a
`(chunk_size, B, dim_model)` tensor fed to the decoder — the same shape the
injection already takes. Four files, about a tenth of what Stage F cost, and it
turns the project's central question into an answerable one.

**3. Stop running VLA cells until something changes.**
Four backbones spanning 45× in parameters, two action encodings, three
conditioning mechanisms: zero successes in 240 episodes. Further cells at this
budget are unlikely to be informative. If VLAs are to be revisited, change the
budget (40 k steps, matching ACT) or the data scale, not the backbone.

**4. Make the compositional claim testable.**
§3.4 hints that conditioning degrades more gracefully with object count. To turn
that into a result: seed replication, plus the now-valid permutation control
(`object_entity_positional=true` at training, shuffle at evaluation), plus
Alfa-d's relational-recombination diagnostic.

**5. Fix the conditioning vector's sparsity.**
88 of 120 dimensions are constant. Either size the token set to the scene or
drop the empty slots before the encoder. Cheap, and it removes the most obvious
confound from every object-conditioning null result above.

**6. Record rollout videos for the unexplained cases.**
Two objects being harder than three, and the 88 % of episodes that lift an
object and never place it. The counters cannot separate "reached and missed"
from "never approached."

### Not recommended

- **Ranking or gating cells on validation loss.** It has failed eight times.
  Use the offline diagnostic as a floor check only.
- **More data as the next lever.** 45→75 runs cut validation loss 30 % and left
  closed-loop at zero.
- **FastWAM, X-VLA, MolmoAct2.** FastWAM at 6 B exceeds the 4090 budget; X-VLA's
  released weights are a transformers `AutoModel` repo that does not meet
  LeRobot's loader; MolmoAct2 at 5.44 B leaves no headroom and may not read
  proprioception at all.
